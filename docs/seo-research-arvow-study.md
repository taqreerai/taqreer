# Arvow content study: why its blog posts earn backlinks and AI citations (2026-10-05)

Goal: learn what works for arvow.com (AI SEO content company, formerly Journalist AI) and
copy the repeatable parts for taqreer.ai (Arabic-first AI presentation tool for teachers and
students in Saudi Arabia, UAE and Kuwait).

Sources: OpenSEO (DataForSEO) for all numbers, plus reading arvow.com pages directly.
Arvow is an English-language site, so its keyword rows use the US market (English). Taqreer
keywords in Part C use Saudi Arabia, Arabic. Where a number could not be pulled it says
"no data".

## Summary (read this first)

1. **Arvow is small.** Estimated organic traffic is about 578 visits a month from 775 ranked
   keywords (US). Backlink rank 48. Its backlink profile is real but mixed with a lot of
   low-quality links.
2. **Its link magnets are statistics roundups and case studies, not its search winners.**
   The 2026 statistics posts and two case studies collect the most referring domains of any
   blog or case-study page, yet none of them appears in arvow's top 100 ranked keywords by
   traffic. They earn links by being quoted, not by ranking. The pages that bring search
   traffic are free tools and odd phrase pages.
3. **People link with the statistic itself as the anchor text.** Examples: "94 percent of
   marketers" (from a builtin.com article, domain rank 82), "95% of content creators",
   "3 hours 48 minutes". A stat in a short, quotable sentence with a named source is the
   unit that gets reused.
4. **The statistics pages are mostly collected data, not original research.** They cite 18 to
   20+ named sources, say how often they update, and put the year in the title.
5. **Arabic "statistics" keywords have almost no search volume** (see Part C). The
   statistics-roundup format is worth copying for link and AI-citation value, but not for
   direct search traffic. Education how-to and definition keywords in Arabic have real
   volume at difficulty 0.

## Part B: why the content works (pages read directly)

Pages read in full: blogging-statistics-2026 and ai-content-marketing-statistics-2026
(statistics roundups), the "$0 to $10,000/mo" case study, the ai-news-article-generator tool
page, and three new posts (does-ai-seo-work, ai-backlink-agent, link-swap). The other
top-linked statistics posts (seo-agency, chatgpt, ai-overviews, programmatic-seo) and the
second case study were not read; they follow the same URL pattern, but I did not verify
their structure.

### B1. Statistics roundups (the main link magnet)

**ai-content-marketing-statistics-2026**
- Title: "AI Content Marketing Statistics (2026): 50+ Data Points on Adoption, ROI, and
  Trends". Year and a count in the title.
- Published about 6 months ago, "last updated March 2026"; the methodology says it is
  refreshed quarterly. About 7,500 words, 12 H2 sections.
- Author byline: Vasco Monteiro, with an X link. No credentials listed.
- Data: collected, not original. Each stat is a bold headline number, a short context
  sentence, and a source label (for example "HubSpot", "McKinsey"). Three-column tables
  (metric, value, source), year-over-year tables (adoption 2022 to 2026), a market-size
  table with growth rates, and a "Key Takeaways" box of 10 stats at the top.
- Methodology section at the bottom lists 18+ sources.
- No FAQ, no question-style headings, no embeddable chart, no "cite this page" box.
- Internal links: 8+ links to product pages (AI Article Writer, Autoblog, visibility tracker).

**blogging-statistics-2026**
- Title: "Blogging Statistics (2026): 60+ Data Points on Traffic, Income, AI, and Trends".
  About 4,500 words. No updated date visible.
- 17 H2 sections (traffic, AI, SEO, income, platforms, niches, demographics, future), no H3.
- Collected data with named sources (Tech Business News, Orbit Media 2025, HubSpot).
  Key Takeaways box, several tables, a methodology and sources section listing 20+ sources.
- Links to product pages: AI Article Writer, AI SEO Agent, Autoblog, visibility tracker.

Why it earns links, from the anchors seen: other bloggers need a number to support a claim
and cite the page as the place where the number is collected. The anchor is the number
("95% of content creators"). A single page holding 50 to 60 current numbers on one topic,
sorted under clear headings, saves each writer the research.

### B2. Case studies

"$0 to $10,000/mo in 3 Months" (about 2,500 words, dated July 2024). Original data: Google
Search Console growth screenshot, SEMrush screenshots, a named customer, one precise result
($0 to $10,000 in 90 days). Includes a "How to replicate this" section and a strategy
section. Linked by blog.hubspot.com (rank 88) using the headline as the anchor. The result
is stated as a single number that is easy to quote.

### B3. Free tool pages

ai-news-article-generator (about 1,200 to 1,400 words): "3 news articles for free" without
signup, a 3-step how-it-works, 13-question FAQ, "trusted by 20,000+" social proof, and the
line that you can embed the generator in your blog. Free, no-signup tools are what
tool-roundup and directory sites link to; the anchors are the tool name.

### B4. Newer posts (original experiments), recent and not yet linked

- Does AI SEO Work? What 33 Customer Sites Show in Ahrefs (2026): ORIGINAL data from
  arvow's own customer database merged with Ahrefs traffic. States the sample rules, a
  33-row data table, and an explicit "What this data can't prove" section. A one-line
  quotable answer near the top. Ends with a 6-step checklist and an FAQ.
- AI Backlink Agent vs a $295 Fiverr gig: original experiment, 14 verified live listings
  on day one; headings are statements; 3-question FAQ.
- Link Swap: a numbered 6-step method, a Do/Skip checklist, an outreach email template, a
  4-question FAQ.

These have no backlink data yet (a few days old), so their link-earning is unproven. The
structure is the strongest of the pages read.

### B5. Why AI engines are likely to quote these pages

Inference from structure (I did not test which AI engines cite arvow, and OpenSEO has no
AI-citation check here): short quotable answers near the top, one fact per sentence with
a named source, tables with explicit labels, key-takeaway boxes, the year in the title,
and FAQ blocks. Arvow also runs an AI visibility tracker product, so it designs for this.

### B6. Checklist to apply to every taqreer.ai page

1. Put the year and a count in the title ("2026", "30+ data points").
2. Open with a 1 to 2 sentence direct answer or a Key Takeaways box.
3. One fact per sentence: number, context, named source. Make the number bold.
4. Cite sources inline AND list them all in a "Methodology and sources" section; say how
   often the page is updated and show "Updated: [date]".
5. Use tables (metric, value, source) and year-over-year comparisons.
6. Prefer ORIGINAL data when you have it (your own anonymized usage) and state the sample,
   the method, and what the data cannot prove.
7. Phrase some headings as the question people type, and end with an FAQ of 3 to 5
   questions (also mark it up as FAQ schema).
8. Name a human author with a short bio and a link.
9. Give a linkable asset: a free no-signup tool, a template, a checklist, or a downloadable
   chart. Add a short "How to cite this page" line with the exact stat wording.
10. Link naturally to 2 to 4 product pages from the text.
11. Keep numbers current; refresh on a schedule and change the date.
12. Do not rely on directory links: arvow's biggest link sources by count are low-quality.

### Suggested order

1. Free tools first (6, 7, 8): they have the best volume at difficulty 0 and the clearest
   link path.
2. Page 3 (استراتيجيات التدريس), the largest teacher keyword, with free decks.
3. Pages 1 and 2 (statistics roundups) once sourced from official data.
4. Page 10 (original data) when a privacy-reviewed aggregate is ready.
